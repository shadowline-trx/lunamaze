New York's allergen labeling law: what food shops have to do

If you make food, pack it and sell it from the same New York location, you will need an allergen notice on the package from November 12, 2026. Here is what the law says, in plain English.

The law
Chapter 494 of the Laws of 2025 (Senate bill S5381B, Assembly bill A6558A). It adds section 1357 to the Public Health Law and section 199-g to the Agriculture and Markets Law.
Signed
November 12, 2025.
Starts
November 12, 2026. The act says it takes effect "one year after it shall have become a law".
Who it covers
Food establishments where food is prepared and sold on the same premises: delis, bakeries, sandwich shops, ice cream parlors, cafeterias, food trucks and retail food stores. The text sets no size limit.
What you must do
Put a written notice on the package, or on a label attached to it, that identifies any ingredient that is a major food allergen.
Not covered
Food that is not in packaging, and food packaged after a customer orders it.

What counts as "prepackaged food"

The act defines it as food that is prepared, packed and offered or sold to customers on the same premises. So the muffins you bag in the morning and sell from the case are covered. So is the salad you box at 7 a.m. for the grab-and-go fridge.

The sandwich you wrap after a customer orders it is not covered, and neither is a slice of cake boxed at the counter after the sale. The rule is about food that is already packed when the customer chooses it.

The nine allergens

Milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, wheat, peanuts, sesame and soy. The act also covers ingredients that contain protein from those foods. It exempts highly refined oils made from those foods, and ingredients the FDA has exempted through the federal allergen law's (FALCPA) petition or notification process.

What the label can look like

The act asks for a written notification that identifies the allergen ingredients. A clear "Contains" line does that. Add the ingredient list with allergens in bold and customers can see exactly where each one comes from.

Blueberry muffin
INGREDIENTS: Enriched flour (wheat), sugar, butter, eggs, milk, blueberries, baking powder, vanilla, salt.
Contains: Milk, Eggs, Wheat
Rosa's Bakery, 12 Main St
Chicken caesar wrap
INGREDIENTS: Chicken, romaine, parmesan, caesar dressing (anchovy, egg, oil, garlic), wheat tortilla.
Contains: Milk, Eggs, Fish, Wheat

Penalties

The act does not set a fine of its own. It puts the rule into two laws, and each has a general penalty section:

  • Agriculture and Markets Law, section 39: a civil penalty of up to $600 for a first violation and up to $1,200 for each later one. This is the law the state uses for the retail food stores it licenses.
  • Public Health Law, section 12: a civil penalty of up to $2,000 per violation, with higher limits for repeat violations that seriously threaten health and for violations that cause serious harm.

Which one applies, and how inspections will work, is up to the Department of Agriculture and Markets and the health departments. Ask your inspector or local health department.

A seven-step checklist for the next few weeks

  1. List every item you pack in advance and sell from the case, shelf or fridge.
  2. For each one, collect the ingredient list from every supplier label. Do not go from memory.
  3. Mark which of the nine allergens each item contains, including hidden ones in sauces, flavorings and chocolate.
  4. Print a label for each item with a clear "Contains" line.
  5. Set a rule that a recipe or supplier change means a label check the same day.
  6. Tell your staff what the labels mean and who owns updating them.
  7. Keep the supplier labels somewhere you can show an inspector.

What we do not know yet

State agencies may publish more detail before the start date on label wording, placement and inspections. Check the Department of Agriculture and Markets and Department of Health before November 12. Other states are moving too: California's SB 68 started for chain restaurants on July 1, 2026, and bills have been introduced in other states.
